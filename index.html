8th Grade Science Unit 3 Study Guide

Unit: Newton's Laws of Motion · TEKS 8.7A and 8.7B

Study Guide

TEKS 8.7A

Newton's Second Law. Calculate and analyze how the acceleration of an object depends on the net force on it and its mass.

TEKS 8.7B

Three laws together. Investigate and describe how Newton's three laws act at the same time in systems such as vehicle restraints, sports, amusement park rides, Earth's tectonic activity and rocket launches.

Formula station

Step 1: find the net force. Add forces that point the same way. Subtract forces that point opposite ways. The net force points toward the larger force.

Step 2: use the formula. Rearrange it for what is missing, and write the unit.

Worked examples

  1. Find acceleration. A 4 kg wagon is pulled right with 30 N while friction pushes left with 10 N. Net force = 30 − 10 = 20 N right. a = 20 N ÷ 4 kg = 5 m/s² to the right.
  2. Find force. A 1,000 kg car speeds up at 3 m/s². F = 1,000 kg × 3 m/s² = 3,000 N.
  3. Find mass. A net force of 36 N gives an object an acceleration of 9 m/s². m = 36 N ÷ 9 m/s² = 4 kg.

Patterns to know

  • Double the net force on the same mass, and the acceleration doubles.
  • Double the mass with the same net force, and the acceleration is cut in half.
  • Balanced forces mean the net force is 0 N, so the acceleration is 0 m/s². The object stays at rest or keeps moving at a constant velocity.
  • Mass and weight are different. Mass (kg) stays the same everywhere. Weight is a force (N) that depends on gravity.

The three laws and their clues

LawIn plain wordsClue in a question
1st (inertia)Motion does not change without an unbalanced force.An object keeps moving, stays still, or resists a change.
2nd (F = ma)More net force or less mass means more acceleration.Speeding up, slowing down, turning, or comparing masses.
3rd (action-reaction)Two objects push on each other with equal and opposite forces.One object pushes or pulls another.

All three laws in real systems (8.7B)

Vehicle restraints: seat belts and airbags

1st: in a sudden stop, a passenger's body keeps moving forward.

2nd: the belt applies a force that slows the passenger. An airbag spreads the stopping over more time, so the force on the body is smaller.

3rd: the passenger pushes on the belt, and the belt pushes back equally.

Sports: kicking or hitting a ball

3rd: the foot pushes on the ball and the ball pushes back on the foot.

2nd: a lighter ball with the same force accelerates more.

1st: after the kick, the ball keeps moving until gravity, air resistance and friction change its motion.

Amusement park rides: roller coasters

2nd: gravity pulling the car down a hill creates a net force, so the car speeds up.

1st: riders tend to keep moving in a straight line, so they feel pushed to the side in a turn.

3rd: the seat pushes on the rider, and the rider pushes on the seat.

Earth's tectonic activity

1st: plates stay locked while friction balances the forces on them.

2nd: when forces build beyond friction, the net force is unbalanced and the rock accelerates, causing an earthquake.

3rd: plates push on each other with equal and opposite forces where they meet.

Rocket launches

3rd: the rocket pushes exhaust gas down, and the gas pushes the rocket up.

2nd: when thrust is greater than the rocket's weight, the net force points up and the rocket accelerates. As fuel burns, the mass drops and the acceleration grows.

1st: in space, with no net force, the rocket coasts at a constant velocity.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Action and reaction forces do NOT cancel, because they act on different objects.
  • An object in motion does not need a net force to keep moving. It needs a net force to change its motion.
  • Friction is a force too. Include it when you find the net force.
